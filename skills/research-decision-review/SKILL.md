# Research Decision Review

Use concentrated review for decisions that change the research claim, protocol, direction or major resource commitment. Keep routine implementation, mechanical checks and work inside an accepted protocol with the investigator.

Read [decision protocol](references/decision-protocol.md) for role/cost rules and [packet template](references/packet-template.md) when preparing a review.

## Establish authority and actual capabilities

Use the project's configured investigator, optional ideation assistant, reviewer and decision owner. Do not hardcode a vendor, model version, CLI or account. The user owns consequential decisions unless they have explicitly delegated a bounded scientific choice.

Check actual CLI/model identifiers and usable routes before claiming a review happened. Installed tools or credentials are not permission for spending or private-data transfer. Preserve existing hard budgets and data-sharing constraints.

## Prepare a decision-ready packet

Complete necessary authorized investigation first. Make one concrete recommendation with two or three viable choices when alternatives matter. Include:
- the exact decision and what would change;
- strongest supporting evidence and counterevidence;
- source/artifact anchors and comparison conditions;
- nearest-mechanism difference when novelty is at stake;
- cost, resource dependencies and stopping conditions;
- missing evidence and the smallest check that would change the decision;
- existing decision authority and permitted execution scope.

Usually a page plus evidence links is sufficient. Do not bury missing experiments inside polished prose. Distinguish measured results, interpretations, planned targets and unknowns.

## Review once per consequential node

Provide only task-needed materials through the approved route. Ask the reviewer for an explicit disposition: adopt, reject, conditional adoption, or obtain named evidence first, with conditions and reasons.

Batch related core issues. Reuse a prior verdict only after checking that its assumptions, artifact versions and decision still match. Request another review only for new evidence, material factual errors, changed conditions or execution results that alter the choice. Do not run repeated agreement-seeking calls.

Preserve requested and resolved model, route, response ID/log, input artifact/version, output, timestamp, usage when available and failures. If the route is unavailable, rate-limited or exhausted, stop attempts, save the packet as pending review and continue only work independent of that decision. Do not claim approval or silently substitute a reviewer.

## Verify, decide and execute within scope

Keep the original review. Check quoted sources, numbers and claimed experiments against raw evidence. Return material factual errors to the reviewer for correction; do not silently alter the original verdict. Reviewer agreement alone is not scientific evidence.

Record the verified recommendation and the actual decision by the authorized owner. A recommendation is not an approved decision. Delegated scientific authority never authorizes external publication, new spending, destructive cleanup or account changes.

Implement only work already authorized. Keep the decision, conditions, evidence versions, observed implementation and remaining issues together. Report what changed and how it was checked.

This skill works without the other skills in this bundle. A fresh reviewer is useful when required by the decision; a cross-model reading campaign is not automatically required for every review.
